1
Step 1: Multi-Format Ingestion & Coordinate Reference Standardization Python: rasterio, spectral, h5py, netCDF4

Raw downloads arrive in disparate formats with different axis interleaving (BIL/BIP/BSQ) and projection systems. This step unifies all inputs into standard in-memory arrays with geodetic coordinates.

Key Operations & Technical Parameters:
  • Axis Re-ordering: Standardize array dimensions to (Channels, Height, Width) regardless of whether the source is Band Interleaved by Line (BIL) or Band Sequential (BSQ).
  • Coordinate Standardization: Reproject spatial bounding boxes from local UTM zones (e.g., UTM 43N / 44N across India) to standard EPSG:4326 (WGS84) and EPSG:3857 (Web Mercator for web rendering).
  • Memory Optimization: Use memory-mapped I/O (numpy.memmap) and chunked windows via rioxarray to ensure 2–4 GB raw scenes load in <1.5 GB RAM without full-file decompression.
💻 Runs on: Local Laptop or AIRAWAT CPU ⏱ Execution Time: ~45–60 sec per flight line
2
Step 2: Physics-Based Bad Band Removal (BBR) & Sensor Edge Truncation Radiometric Calibration

Solar radiation is heavily absorbed by atmospheric gases ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$), leaving zero-SNR "blind zones". Retaining these noisy bands introduces extreme variance into downstream AI models.

Spectral Filtering Rules:
  • Water Vapor Absorption Windows (Removed):
    • $\alpha$-band: 1350 nm – 1420 nm (Deep atmospheric moisture opacity)
    • $\rho\sigma\tau$-band: 1800 nm – 1950 nm (Water vapor + atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$)
  • Detector Edge Artifacts (Removed): Strip noisy channels at extreme limits: < 400 nm (low sensor quantum efficiency) and > 2450 nm (thermal noise).
  • Channel Dimension Reduction: Reduces raw 425 bands (AVIRIS-NG) or 285 bands (NASA EMIT) down to 200–224 clean, high-SNR scientific channels.
💻 Runs on: Local Laptop or AIRAWAT CPU ⏱ Execution Time: ~15 sec per scene
3
Step 3: Bitwise Quality Masking, Radiometric Scaling & Anomaly Cleaning Quality Control & Normalization

Spaceborne sensors provide companion quality assurance (QA) rasters containing bitwise flags for clouds, cirrus, cloud shadows, and detector saturation.

Quality & Normalization Rules:
  • Bitmask Application: Evaluate bit flags in EMITL2AMASK or EnMAP QA layers to set cloud-covered and shadow pixels to NaN.
  • Bad Pixel Handling: Identify dead detector pixels (values ≤ 0 or > 10,000) and perform spatial neighborhood median interpolation.
  • Dynamic Range Normalization: Convert 16-bit integer Digital Numbers (DN scaled $\times 10,000$) to continuous 32-bit floating point surface reflectance $\rho \in [0.0, 1.0]$.
💻 Runs on: Local Laptop or AIRAWAT CPU ⏱ Execution Time: ~20 sec per scene
4
Step 4: Spatial-Spectral Patching & Sliding Window Structuring Deep Learning Tiling

Full hyperspectral scenes (e.g. 10,000 × 800 pixels) cannot fit in GPU VRAM during training. They are decomposed into standardized spatial-spectral patches.

Patching & Geometry Rules:
  • Tile Dimensions: Decompose continuous flight strips into non-overlapping or 50%-strided $128 \times 128 \times 224$ or $64 \times 64 \times 224$ sub-cubes.
  • Background Filtering: Automatically discard tiles where $>30\%$ of pixels contain clouds, water (unless studying water quality), or nodata padding.
  • Spatial Metadata Stamping: Every patch preserves its unique affine transform matrix and corner bounding box for seamless reassembly after inference.
💻 Runs on: Local Laptop or AIRAWAT CPU ⏱ Execution Time: ~1–2 min per flight line
5
Step 5: Output Serialization into Analysis-Ready Datacubes Final Storage Artifacts

The pipeline terminates at three standardized, cloud-native storage artifacts that decouple data preparation from AI modeling and web serving.

The 3 Output Artifacts Produced:
  • 1. Standardized Zarr Datacubes (.zarr): Compressed multi-dimensional N-D arrays chunked along (chunk_y=128, chunk_x=128, chunk_b=32) with Blosc/Zstandard compression. Enables lightning-fast distributed parallel reads during AIRAWAT training.
  • 2. Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COGs): Geotagged rasters with internal overview pyramids (downsampled zoom levels) optimized for HTTP Range requests on Cloudflare R2.
  • 3. Tabular Spectral Signature Reference (.parquet / .csv): Calibrated pure-pixel reflectance curves mapped to known field coordinates for baseline spectral angle mapping (SAM) and unmixing priors.
💻 Output Location: Local Storage / Cloudflare R2 / Scratch Disk

💡 Key Strategic Takeaway for the Team:
Data downloading and the 5 preprocessing steps are strictly CPU and Disk I/O bound. They do not require GPUs at all. Because we stream and chunk data using rioxarray and zarr, the memory footprint never exceeds ~2.5 GB.

Therefore, you can run all data preprocessing on your local laptop or on the basic AIRAWAT 7GB CPU environment immediately. Save your 4-hour GPU windows (and the upcoming 20GB tier) exclusively for training the heavy Deep Learning Transformer / 3D-CNN models.
