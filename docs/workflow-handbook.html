1. The Session Lifecycle & Architecture

Understanding how our ephemeral web sessions persist your work.

Why this architecture?

AIRAWAT sessions are ephemeral. When you request a CPU/GPU web session, you are assigned a clean container. If we relied on manual setup, you would spend hours installing PyTorch and GDAL dependencies every session.

Instead, the project lead has built a Golden Environment. By running a single initialization command at the top of your notebook, your environment is perfectly configured, your previous code is restored from R2, and your AI tutor's memory is synced. When you are done, one command saves everything safely back to the cloud.

A unified stack for hyperspectral machine learning.

🐍 Core ML & Geospatial

  • Python 3.10 (Strict pinned dependencies)
  • PyTorch 2.x (with AMP / bfloat16 for A100 Tensor Cores)
  • Rasterio & RioXarray (Windowed geospatial raster reading)
  • Spectral Python (SPy) (Hyperspectral algorithms)

☁️ Storage & Big Data

  • Cloudflare R2 ($0 Egress S3-compatible object storage)
  • s3fs / fsspec (Direct filesystem hooks to R2)
  • Zarr & Xarray (Chunked N-dimensional datacubes)
  • Dask (Out-of-core calculations to prevent RAM crashes)

3. The Code Harness: agy cli

Your personalized, stateful AI tutor for navigating the codebase.

As you work through notebooks in your browser, you will also open a Web Terminal tab inside JupyterLab. Here, you will run agy, our custom Antigravity CLI agent harness.

Socratic Custom Skills: The agent has been pre-configured with project-specific skills (e.g., hyperspectral-tutor, r2-data-streamer). It is designed to act as your tutor. If you don't understand why we remove certain atmospheric bands, ask the agent. It knows the context of our project and will guide you step-by-step.

# In your Jupyter Web Terminal, simply type:
$ agy

# The agent is loaded with our project's context. Try asking:
> "Explain why we drop the 1350–1420 nm bands in Phase 1, and check my tensor shape."

Because of our btp_session.snapshot() pipeline, the agent remembers your conversations across sessions. It knows what you learned yesterday and will adapt its guidance accordingly.

5. The Pedagogical Roadmap

How we build up to the core intelligence of the system.

🌱 Phases 1 & 2: Independent Hands-On Learning

You will complete these phases on your own, guided by the interactive notebooks and the agy tutor. You will learn to stream AVIRIS-NG datasets from R2, apply Bad Band Removal (BBR), map spectral signatures (like the chlorophyll red-edge), and build baseline 1D/2D PyTorch classifiers. By the end, hyperspectral data formats and dimensions $(H \times W \times B)$ will become second nature to you.

🧠 Phase 3: The Creative Core (Foundation Models)

Once the team is fluent in the basics, we unite for the heaviest and most critical phase: Phase 3. Here, we move beyond tutorials. We will collaboratively design a novel Spatial-Spectral Masked Autoencoder (Spectral-MAE). This involves 3D patch tokenization, attention mechanisms across hundreds of bands, and Knowledge Distillation to compress heavy models. This is where the true innovation of the project is forged.

🚀 Phases 4 & 5: Platform Engineering & Edge Deployment

With the intelligence artifacts forged in Phase 3, the final phases transition into heavy software engineering (Cloudflare WebGIS platforms, R2 tile streaming APIs, and Google Coral INT8 edge deployments). The project lead will take primary ownership of the Phase 4 & 5 deployment execution, allowing the rest of the team to focus purely on the machine learning research outcomes.

🎯

The Goal of this Workflow

By automating environment setup and state management, we eliminate "DevOps fatigue." You can focus 100% of your energy on learning hyperspectral ML concepts, leaning heavily on the custom agent harness to guide you, so we are all operating at peak capability when the demanding Phase 3 begins.
