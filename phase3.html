🌟 1. The Paradigm Shift: From Baseline Classifiers to Foundation AI
Overcoming the label scarcity bottleneck in Indian remote sensing through self-supervised pre-training

🧠 Why Supervised Learning Alone Fails at National Scale

In Phase 2, our baseline models (1D-CNN, 2D-CNN, and 3D HybridSN) demonstrated strong classification accuracy when trained on well-annotated benchmarks. However, in real-world Indian agricultural landscapes—such as Punjab paddy fields, Gujarat cotton farms, and Indo-Gangetic stubble belts—high-quality ground-truth annotations cover less than 0.1% of the geographical area. Supervised models trained from scratch severely overfit or collapse when annotated samples drop below 20 samples per class.

🔮

Self-Supervised Pre-Training

By masking 75%–80% of spatial-spectral tokens across hundreds of gigabytes of unlabelled AVIRIS-NG and EMIT scenes, the model learns the fundamental physics of light interaction, molecular bond vibrations, and continuous spectral signatures without a single human label.

Spectral-MAE 3D Tokenization Unsupervised
🎯

Few-Shot Downstream Transfer

When transferring the pre-trained encoder to downstream agricultural tasks (crop stage discrimination, nitrogen stress detection, soil carbon estimation), the model achieves >93% accuracy with only 5% labeled data, outperforming from-scratch baselines by over 15 percentage points.

Heavy Vision Transformers (>50M parameters, 4GB VRAM) cannot be directly served with low latency in citizen web apps or field devices. Teacher-student distillation transfers the rich latent “dark knowledge” into an ultra-lean 3D-2D CNN student (<1.8M params).

🔬 3. Deep Dive: Spectral-MAE Architecture & Tensor Mechanics
Spatial-spectral patch tokenization, factorized 3D positional embeddings, and asymmetric encoder-decoder dynamics
1
3D Patch Tokenization & Factorized Positional Encoding
Stage 1: Pre-Training

Standard 2D Vision Transformers treat each spatial patch as a flat RGB token. Hyperspectral cubes contain 200+ continuous spectral bands with high inter-band correlation. We partition the cube X in R^(H x W x B) into non-overlapping 3D cuboids of size P_H x P_W x P_C = 4 x 4 x 16.

📐 3D Patch Token Formulation & Count
N = (H / P_H) × (W / P_W) × (B / P_C) = (64 / 4) × (64 / 4) × (208 / 16) = 16 × 16 × 13 = 3,328 Tokens
E_pos(h, w, c) = E_spatial(h, w) + E_spectral(c), where E_spectral in R^D is indexed by central band wavelength λ_c
P_H, P_W = 4: Spatial patch resolution (sub-field farm boundary granularity)
P_C = 16: Spectral group depth preserving narrow chemical absorption curves
D = 768: Latent embedding dimension (ViT-Base standard)
E_pos: 3D Factorized positional encoding disentangling spatial coordinate from spectral wavelength
Input Cube Shape 64 × 64 × 208 bands
3D Patch Kernel Conv3D (4×4×16, stride 4×4×16)
Total Token Grid 3,328 tokens / cube
Visible Tokens (75% Masked) 832 tokens (25% processed)
2
High-Ratio 3D Masking & Asymmetric Transformer Encoder-Decoder
Compute Efficiency

Because adjacent spectral channels and neighboring spatial pixels contain substantial redundancy, standard 15% masking allows trivial linear interpolation. Applying a 75%–80% uniform random masking ratio forces the encoder to learn deep physical absorption relationships. Since self-attention scales quadratically O(N²), operating strictly on the 25% visible tokens yields a massive 16× compute speedup.

⚡ The Asymmetric Compute Advantage

Heavy Encoder (ViT-Base): 12 Transformer blocks, 768 hidden dimension, 12 attention heads (~86M parameters). Operates strictly on the 832 visible tokens, completely omitting masked tokens from GPU memory during self-attention computation.
Lightweight Decoder: 4 Transformer blocks, 384 hidden dimension, 8 attention heads (~14M parameters). Visible latent embeddings are concatenated with learnable [MASK] tokens and full 3D positional embeddings to reconstruct raw 3D voxel reflectance values across all 3,328 patch locations.

Masking Ratio 75.0% (Uniform 3D Random)
Encoder Architecture ViT-Base (12 Layers, 12 Heads, D=768)
Decoder Architecture Lightweight ViT (4 Layers, 8 Heads, D=384)
Attention FLOP Reduction 16× ( (1 / 0.25)² = 16 )
3
Composite Spectral Reconstruction Loss (MSE + Spectral Angle Distance)
Loss Formulation

Standard pixel-level MSE loss only measures Euclidean amplitude error. In hyperspectral imaging, atmospheric haze, cloud shadows, and solar incident angles scale the absolute reflectance up or down without altering chemical composition. We combine Mean Squared Error (MSE) with Spectral Angle Distance (SAD) to guarantee illumination-invariant spectral shape recovery.

📐 Composite Reconstruction Loss Function
L_reconstruct = MSE(X_masked, X̂_masked) + λ_SAD · SAD(X_masked, X̂_masked)
SAD(x, x̂) = arccos( (x · x̂) / ( ‖x‖₂ · ‖x̂‖₂ + ε ) ) [or Cosine Loss: 1 - (x · x̂) / (‖x‖₂‖x̂‖₂)]
MSE(·): Penalizes absolute amplitude and radiometric reconstruction errors
SAD(·): Enforces correct spectral curvature, preserving chlorophyll red-edge & SWIR absorption dips
λ_SAD = 0.35: Optimal balancing hyperparameter empirically validated on AVIRIS-NG scenes
ε = 1e-7: Numerical stability epsilon preventing division by zero

🎓 5. Teacher-to-Student Knowledge Distillation Protocol
Compressing the 86M-parameter Transformer Teacher into a 1.8M-parameter Mobile 3D-2D CNN

💡 The Distillation Mechanism: Transferring “Dark Knowledge”

While the heavy Transformer Teacher captures subtle inter-class spectral correlations (e.g., how closely sugarcane reflectance resembles early-stage maize in SWIR bands), serving an 86M parameter model in production requires high GPU VRAM. The Student Network is trained on a three-part composite loss that forces it to emulate the teacher's softened probability distribution (τ = 4.0) and match intermediate latent feature activations via 1×1 conv projections.

📐 Composite Distillation Loss Formulation
L_distill = α · L_task(y, ŷ_s) + (1 - α) · τ² · D_KL( σ(z_s / τ) ‖ σ(z_t / τ) ) + β · ‖ W_proj(F_s) - F_t ‖₂²
L_task: Standard Cross-Entropy loss against hard ground-truth labels y
D_KL(·): Kullback-Leibler divergence between temperature-softened student logits z_s and teacher logits z_t
τ = 4.0: Softening temperature revealing subtle inter-class spectral similarities
α = 0.3: Task weight giving 70% gradient importance to the teacher's dark knowledge
W_proj(F_s): 1×1 Convolution projecting student intermediate channels to match teacher latent tokens F_t
β = 0.5: Intermediate feature hint alignment coefficient

⚡ 6. AIRAWAT DGX A100 Distributed Compute & Slurm Strategy
Multi-GPU DistributedDataParallel (DDP), bfloat16 mixed precision, and Slurm 4-hour session management
🚀

PyTorch DDP Distributed Scaling

Distributed training across 4× NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs via torchrun --nproc_per_node=4. Per-GPU batch size is set to 32 cubes (effective global batch size = 128 cubes per step), delivering a sustained pre-training throughput of ~420 patches/second.

PyTorch 2.x DDP Effective Batch = 128
💎

bfloat16 & Gradient Checkpointing

Utilizing torch.cuda.amp.autocast(dtype=torch.bfloat16) for native A100 Tensor Core throughput without numerical underflow. PyTorch activation gradient checkpointing on Transformer blocks maintains VRAM usage under 18 GB per GPU.

bfloat16 AMP < 18 GB VRAM / GPU
⏱️

4-Hour Slurm Session Strategy

C-DAC AIRAWAT enforces a strict 4-hour Slurm job wall-time limit. The training loop executes automated stateful checkpointing every 25 epochs. Re-submitting the Slurm script seamlessly triggers --resume_from checkpoint_epoch_XX.pt without manual intervention.

Stateful Checkpointing Zero Progress Loss
