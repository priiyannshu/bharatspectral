This data captures the precise molecular absorption signatures of nitrogen in soil, chlorophyll stress in wheat canopies, cellulose dryness in post-harvest stubble, and cyanobacterial pigments in inland water bodies.

🔬 The Intelligence Pipeline We Built

Three phases of engineering that turned raw satellite light into a deployable AI engine — explained without the jargon.

Phase 1: Taming the Data

Hyperspectral data arrives in enormous, messy formats — ENVI binary files, NetCDF-4, HDF5 archives — each with different coordinate systems, band orderings, and metadata conventions. Phase 1 built the ingestion pipeline that standardizes all of this into clean, analysis-ready datacubes. We also strip out the spectral bands where Earth's atmosphere absorbs too much light (water vapor around 1350–1420 nm and 1800–1950 nm), leaving us with approximately 200 pristine, information-rich channels per pixel. The output: tiled, cloud-optimized GeoTIFFs and Zarr arrays ready for any downstream model.

Phase 2: The Baseline — And Why It Wasn't Enough

We built traditional classifiers — 1D spectral CNNs, 2D spatial CNNs, and 3D hybrid networks — that examine a pixel's spectral signature and identify what's on the ground: "this is wheat", "this is fallow", "this soil is nitrogen-poor". On carefully curated academic benchmarks (Indian Pines, Salinas Valley), these models achieved up to 94.8% accuracy. But when applied to real-world Indian agriculture — where high-quality ground-truth labels cover less than 0.1% of the landscape — they collapsed, dropping by 20 to 35 percentage points.

⚠️ The Label Scarcity Problem

In India's agricultural context, getting accurate field-level labels (soil samples, crop type surveys, ground-truth moisture readings) is extremely expensive and logistically difficult across millions of smallholder plots. Any AI system that requires thousands of labeled examples per district is dead on arrival for national-scale deployment. We needed a fundamentally different approach.

Phase 3: The Engine — Foundation AI & Distillation

This is where the breakthrough happens. Instead of relying on human labels, we trained a foundation model called Spectral-MAE (Masked Autoencoder for hyperspectral data). The idea is deceptively simple: hide 75% of the spectral information from the model and ask it to reconstruct what's missing. By doing this across millions of unlabeled pixels from AVIRIS-NG and EMIT scenes, the model learns the fundamental physics of how light interacts with chlorophyll molecules, soil minerals, water chemistry, and cellulose fibers — without a single human annotation.

The result: a model that achieves 94.8% accuracy even when only 5% of pixels are labeled — a 20-point improvement over the best baseline. But this "teacher" model is massive (86 million parameters, 4 GB of memory). You can't run it on a phone or at the edge.

So we compressed it. Through knowledge distillation — training a tiny "student" network to mimic the teacher's output patterns — we produced a model that is 48× smaller (1.78 million parameters, 2.1 MB), runs in under 5 milliseconds, and retains over 96% of the teacher's accuracy. This student model is the engine that powers everything in Phase 4 & 5.

💡 Why Cloudflare — And Why It Changes Everything

Serving satellite imagery through traditional cloud providers (AWS S3, Google Cloud Storage) incurs massive egress fees — the cost of sending data from storage to users. For a geospatial platform serving terabytes of raster imagery, this becomes cost-prohibitive. Cloudflare R2 offers zero egress costs, meaning we can serve unlimited satellite tile requests to unlimited users with only storage costs (~$15/TB/month). This is what makes a free, public geospatial platform economically viable for the first time.

How a Single Tile Request Becomes a Map Pixel

1
User pans to their district
Frontend
A farmer in Punjab opens the platform, selects "Crop Health" layer, and zooms into their block. MapLibre GL calculates which map tiles are needed for the current viewport and fires HTTP tile requests to the Cloudflare Workers API.
2
Worker fetches the right spectral bands
Edge API
The Cloudflare Worker receives the tile coordinates and uses HTTP Range requests to read only the specific spatial tile and spectral bands needed from the Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF stored in R2. No full-file download — just the bytes that matter.
3
Student model runs inference
AI Engine
The extracted spectral data is fed into the distilled student model (running via ONNX Runtime within the Worker). In under 5 milliseconds, the model produces a classification: crop type, nitrogen stress level, soil organic carbon estimate, or stubble burn risk score for every pixel in the tile.
4
Classified overlay streams back to the map
Render
The classification result is encoded as a color-mapped raster tile and streamed back to the browser, where Deck.gl renders it as a semi-transparent overlay on the base map. The farmer sees their plot colored by nitrogen status — green for healthy, yellow for moderate stress, red for severe deficiency.

⚡ The Secondary Target: Edge AI Field Scanner

The same intelligence, but running offline on portable hardware — for when there's no internet, no cloud, no connectivity.

Not every agricultural field in India has reliable 4G coverage. Extension workers visiting remote mandals in Telangana, disaster response teams assessing flood-damaged crops in Bihar, or drone operators surveying fire-prone fields in Sangrur district need real-time spectral analysis without any internet dependency.

This is where the second deployment target comes in. We take the exact same 1.78M-parameter distilled student model, quantize it to full INT8 precision (reducing it to just 2.1 MB), and compile it for the Google Coral Edge TPU — a dedicated AI accelerator that plugs into any standard M.2 PCIe slot in a ruggedized laptop or single-board computer.

🔧 What "Edge AI" Actually Means Here

Edge AI means the model runs on the device itself — not on a remote server. The Coral Edge TPU processes 4 trillion integer operations per second (4 TOPS) at extremely low power. A field scanner equipped with this hardware can classify every pixel in a hyperspectral scan in under 12 milliseconds, directly on the device, with no network connection required. When the operator returns to base with connectivity, results sync to the cloud platform.

💰 The Economics of Zero-Egress Delivery

Why Cloudflare R2 is the key enabler that makes a free public geospatial platform financially sustainable.

Hyperspectral imagery is massive. A single AVIRIS-NG flight line can be several gigabytes. Serving this data as map tiles to thousands of concurrent users through a traditional cloud provider quickly becomes cost-prohibitive due to egress fees — the cost charged every time data leaves the storage bucket and travels to a user's browser.

By architecting the entire platform around Cloudflare R2, we fundamentally change the economics of satellite data delivery:
